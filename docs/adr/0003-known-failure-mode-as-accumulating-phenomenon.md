# Known Failure Mode is a named phenomenon with accumulating Evidence, not a flat post

Every other Community Annotation subtype (Mechanistic Finding, Bayesian Annotation) is flat and standalone: posted once, live immediately, no append, no threading — a disagreement becomes its own new entry. We diverged from that for Known Failure Mode: it's now a curator-named phenomenon (e.g. "Systematic PES softening") that accumulates a list of Evidence items — literature citations and community anecdotes alike — over time, and the community can add Evidence to an existing phenomenon rather than only ever posting a new standalone one.

**Why:** this was forced by real data. A literature survey of MACE-MP-0 (`failure_modes/mace_mp0_failure_modes.json`) arrived structured as named phenomena each citing multiple independent findings — not as flat one-off posts — and failure modes genuinely do recur: five reports of "defect energies too low" across different structures and methods are evidence for one phenomenon, not five unrelated claims. Forcing literature content into flat standalone posts would have discarded that grouping for no benefit. The flat pattern still fits Mechanistic Finding and Bayesian Annotation, where there's no shared phenomenon for independent findings to recur under.

**Considered options:** keep Known Failure Mode flat and explode the literature file's findings into ~20 ungrouped standalone posts — rejected, it throws away real structure in the source data and loses the named-phenomenon grouping that makes the feed useful. Naming open to anyone (not curator-only) — rejected for the same reason Model Pages are curator-only to create: an uncurated, duplicate-prone taxonomy of phenomenon names undermines the Hub's core value more than a closed naming policy limits community input, since the community can still always attach Evidence to whatever phenomenon already exists.
